The Supply Room

You slip into the supply room and pull the door shut just as the doctor's footsteps pass the entrance. The narrow space is almost completely dark, illuminated only by a thin strip of light beneath the door. You press your back against the wall and hold your breath, listening as the footsteps move farther down the corridor. For several seconds, the only sound is the faint buzzing of a damaged ceiling light. Then the doctor stops outside the room. The handle begins to turn. You retreat between two rows of rusted metal shelves, knocking over an empty bottle in the process. It strikes the floor with a sharp clatter. The handle stops moving. You hear the doctor inhale slowly on the other side of the door, followed by a quiet chuckle. "I know you're in there." His footsteps move away again, but you do not immediately relax. Instead, you begin examining the room. The shelves are covered with dusty bottles, cracked specimen jars, and stacks of patient records tied together with deteriorating string. A wheeled medical cart sits overturned in one corner, its drawers hanging open. Beneath it, you discover a small ring of keys and a handheld radio with a cracked screen. The radio has a weak battery, but its indicator light flashes occasionally, suggesting that it may still function. You turn it over and notice a hospital maintenance label with several handwritten numbers. Nearby, a message has been scrawled across the wall in black marker. NEVER LET HIM REACH THE BASEMENT. The letters are uneven, as if whoever wrote them was struggling to keep their hand steady. Beneath the warning, someone has scratched a rough map of the hospital's lower floors. Most of the writing is illegible, but three labels remain clear: boiler room, service tunnel, and basement access. A thick red line connects the boiler room to a small symbol that resembles a control panel. You pocket the keys and lift the radio to your ear. Somewhere behind the wall, machinery begins to rumble. A series of pipes vibrates overhead, followed by three slow knocks from the other side of the room. There is no door there, only a solid concrete wall. You now have a possible way to communicate, a set of keys, and a warning that suggests the basement holds something far more dangerous than the doctor. But the footsteps have stopped outside again, and the door handle begins to move.
