The Nurses' Station

You hurry into the nurses' station and shut the half-open door behind you. The room is cluttered with abandoned charts, overturned chairs, and computer monitors that flicker intermittently despite the hospital's apparent lack of power. A cracked wall clock hangs above the counter, its hands frozen at 3:17. The smell of stale coffee mixes with the familiar odor of disinfectant, and several patient files lie scattered across the floor as though someone searched through them in a panic. You move behind the counter and begin sorting through the paperwork. Most of the records are damaged by moisture, but one folder remains intact. Its label reads Security Procedures and Restricted Access. Inside, you find a rough floor plan of Blackwood Hospital, including the location of the main entrance, the laundry exit, the boiler room, and several locked corridors. Red markings indicate areas that have been sealed off from the rest of the building. A handwritten note has been attached to the map. "The front entrance is alarmed. Do not attempt to leave through the main lobby. The laundry exit leads to the loading yard, but the exterior gate may be locked. Maintenance personnel can override the gate from the control panel near the boiler room." Beneath the note, you discover a second document containing a list of names. Several have been crossed out, while others are accompanied by dates and short handwritten remarks. Near the bottom of the page, you find your own name. The entry is dated several years ago, but the accompanying photograph is unmistakably you. A note beside it reads, Patient remembers previous events. Observation required. Your hands begin to shake. You turn to the next page and discover a series of reports describing unexplained power failures, missing medical staff, and repeated attempts by patients to access the basement. Every report ends with the same signature: the doctor's. A noise echoes from the corridor. "You're looking in the wrong place," the doctor calls out. His voice sounds close enough that you instinctively duck beneath the counter. You hear him enter the nurses' station and slowly move between the desks. Papers rustle as he examines the scattered files. He pauses beside the counter, and you can see the tips of his shoes through the gap beneath the cabinets. "I can explain everything," he says. "You just have to come with me." You notice two possible routes. A narrow door leads toward the service corridors, while a maintenance hatch opens beneath the counter. You also have the security map, which may help you reach the loading yard without triggering the front entrance alarm. The doctor places something on the counter above you. It is your patient file. You look down at the copy in your hands and realize that the two documents are identical. What do you do?
